That shadow standing in the corner of your bedroom during sleep paralysis? It isn't a demon. It's a timing glitch inside your brain, and it happens to you every single night.
Normally, when you enter REM sleep, the stage where you dream, your brainstem flips a biological safety switch called REM atonia.
It floods your spinal cord with two chemicals, GABA and glycine, shutting down your voluntary muscles. Why? So you don't physically act out your dreams and hurt yourself.
But sometimes, your conscious mind wakes up before your brainstem turns that lock off. Therefore, you are fully awake, trapped inside a paralyzed body.
And here's the terrifying part: your brain's fear center, the amygdala, immediately panics because you can't move.
At the same time, your brain is still half-stuck in dream mode. So it tries to explain the terror by projecting a vivid dream directly onto your real bedroom, creating the shadow figure and the crushing weight on your chest.
Within seconds, the brainstem catches up, the chemicals clear, and the monster vanishes.
Your brain didn't just trap you. It literally hallucinated a threat to explain its own mistake. And it's not the only time your brain edits reality right in front of your eyes.
